Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 19, 2026

Summary

Tappy is a macOS utility that provides local auditory feedback in response to physical typing activity across your Mac. Tappy does not record, sell, or transmit your typed text.

What Tappy accesses

Tappy uses macOS Input Monitoring so it can trigger local auditory feedback while you type in other apps.

  • Tappy listens for physical key codes and modifier keys to choose which local feedback cue to play.
  • Tappy does not read the characters or words you type.
  • Tappy does not record or store the text you type as user content.
  • Tappy does not upload your typing to a remote server.
  • You can turn off Input Monitoring for Tappy at any time in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Input Monitoring.

Data collection

Tappy does not require account creation and does not use a backend service for core functionality.

  • No account data is collected by Tappy itself.
  • No cloud sync is required for sound packs or settings.
  • Starting with version 1.3.0, Tappy offers optional usage analytics, off by default. If you enable it, Tappy sends a random installation identifier and app interaction events, such as sound pack selections, preview use, setup progress, and purchase or restore actions and access outcomes, to PostHog. You can turn it off in Tappy at any time.
  • Analytics do not include typed text, key codes, or individual keystrokes. PostHog receives network information such as your IP address when events are sent.

Local data

Tappy stores basic local preferences on your Mac, such as the currently selected feedback pack and whether you opted in to analytics. If you opt in, Tappy stores a random installation identifier; opting out deletes it locally. Events previously received by PostHog remain subject to its retention and deletion settings.

Third-party services

Apple processes App Store purchases and in-app purchases under its own privacy policies. If you enable optional analytics, PostHog processes those analytics events. For purchases made through older direct-download versions, Stripe may process purchase-related data under its own policies.
